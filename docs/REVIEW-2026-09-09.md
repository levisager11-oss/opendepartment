# Review — 9 September 2026

A second pass over the whole app, taking [AUDIT.md](../AUDIT.md) as read. That
audit's findings are not repeated here; this covers what it did not reach. The
baseline was commit `43ecb85`.

Seven findings were fixed in the same change as this document. The rest are
recorded rather than fixed, with what each would take.

## Fixed

| Finding | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Anyone holding a department's public URL and anon key could register a second slug against the same project — a live mirror with its own directory entry, and a way past suspension, since `status` is a flag on one row. | `departments_one_project` trigger and exclusive index in [db/control-plane.sql](../db/control-plane.sql) |
| Storage returns the Content-Type the uploader supplied, so an object filed as `application/pdf` could be stored as `text/html` and executed by the `<object>` element in a nested browsing context on the tenant's own supabase.co origin. | Sandboxed `<iframe>` in [FileViewer.tsx](../src/components/dept/FileViewer.tsx); `object-src 'none'` in [middleware.ts](../src/middleware.ts) |
| Nothing anywhere signed a person out of their OpenDepartment account — the session that registers departments and can authorise a project deletion. | [AccountSignOut.tsx](../src/components/account/AccountSignOut.tsx) |
| Signed exhibit URLs lived an hour and cannot be revoked, so deletion and banning both lagged by up to that long. | `SIGNED_URL_TTL` in [types.ts](../src/lib/tenant/types.ts) |
| The Ko-fi widget script loaded on marketing pages before any consent, contradicting the cookie notice's own "no third-party tag", and its `getHTML()` went into `innerHTML`. | [KofiButton.tsx](../src/components/KofiButton.tsx), privacy copy in [legal.ts](../src/lib/legal.ts) |
| Two administrators banning each other concurrently could leave a department with nobody who can moderate it, unrecoverable without the project owner's SQL editor. | Settings lock and outcome check in `admin_set_flag` |
| No member could leave a department or erase what they had filed, and no operator could sign out. | `leave_department()` and `/d/<slug>/account` |

Departments also record which version of `db/tenant-schema.sql` they have
actually run, in a `public.schema_version` row stamped by the last statement in
that file. Until now nothing could tell: the app discovered an out-of-date
department one missing function at a time, by the error, while a corrected
policy — the kind of change the file is versioned for — produced no symptom at
all. Administrators now get a strip under the header, the current SQL on the
administration screen, and a badge on the operator's account page.

A founder signup with a mistyped address claims the department and consumes the
verifier before the address is ever confirmed, and the reissue SQL is guarded on
`not claimed` so it cannot recover it. That one needs the project owner's own
SQL editor by design; the recovery is now written down in
[AUDIT-BOOTSTRAP.md](AUDIT-BOOTSTRAP.md).

## Open

### Unlisted departments are enumerable

`slug_available()` is granted to `anon` and answers for every registered slug,
listed or not. `report_department()` gives a second oracle: `NO_SUCH_DEPARTMENT`
against silence. A wordlist enumerates every archive on the platform, including
the ones whose owners deliberately kept them out of `/directory`.

The availability check has to answer before an account exists — that is the
point of it, and it is the first useful thing the wizard does. So the choice is
between rate-limiting it per address, gating it behind a control-plane session
at the cost of the wizard's first step, and accepting it while wording
README's "unlisted" claim to match: not indexed, not secret.

### Storage admission still trusts the client

`AUDIT.md` leaves the quota bypass open and its reasoning stands. Two related
things are worth naming alongside it now:

- The object's Content-Type is client-supplied. The sandbox above makes that
  stop mattering for rendering, but the value is still whatever the uploader
  said. A storage INSERT policy testing `metadata->>'mimetype'` may be able to
  fence it; whether `metadata` is populated at the point RLS runs was not
  established here, and a policy that is wrong about that refuses every upload.
- A member's outstanding signed URLs now expire in ten minutes rather than an
  hour, which shortens the window after a deletion or a ban without closing it.
  Only a shorter TTL or a proxy closes it, and a proxy contradicts the property
  the README sells: files go browser to bucket and never pass through us.

### Thumbnails are the full originals

`VaultBrowser` mints signed URLs for every image on the page and hands them to
`FileCard` unresized. A 24-card page can be hundreds of megabytes out of the
department owner's free tier, per visitor, per page — and the owner is the
person who finds out. Generating a ~400px WebP in the browser at upload time
and storing it beside the original fits the existing architecture exactly: no
server, no egress through us, one more object per exhibit.

### Administration has no pagination

The lists now say when they are showing a subset, which was the misleading
half. They still cannot show the rest: files cap at 500, reports at 200 each
and the audit log at 100, and reading past that means the Supabase dashboard.
The audit log is the one that matters — it is the accountability record and
there is no export.

### Only images are scrubbed

`scrubImage` strips EXIF before the bytes leave the browser, which is the right
instinct and the right place. PDFs carry author and producer, and video and
audio carry device and sometimes location, and both upload untouched — while
the form's "metadata removed" notice reads as a general promise.

### The abuse queue is write-only

`report_department()` writes to `abuse_reports` and nothing in the app reads it
back. Triage means opening the control plane's Supabase dashboard, and nothing
notifies anybody that a report arrived. The 25-open-reports cap also means a
department with 25 junk reports silently discards genuine ones — the function
returns quietly, on purpose, so the person filing gets no signal either.

### Smaller

- The file page mints two signed URLs per view, one of them for a download link
  most visitors never click.
- `departments.verified_at` is declared and never written or read.
- `department_identity()` publishes `operator_contact` to `anon`, correctly —
  an imprint only members can read is not an imprint — which also makes every
  department's contact address scrapeable from an unauthenticated RPC. Worth
  saying next to the field in the administration screen.
- The signup form's fallback error path still surfaces the raw Supabase
  message when nothing in `mapAuthError` recognises it.

## Second pass — fixed since

Schema version 2, and the first real exercise of the version notice added in
version 1.

- **The vault scales now.** A generated `tsvector` column with a GIN index
  (`to_tsvector('simple', …)`, no extension needed) replaces three `ilike
  '%…%'` scans, and `subject_ids` on the view replaces the id list the browser
  used to fetch and send back. The search term is tokenised on an allowlist
  into a prefix tsquery, so it is a filter *value* rather than syntax spliced
  into a PostgREST `or=(…)` grammar — and a half-typed word still matches,
  which `websearch_to_tsquery` alone would have broken. Filenames are split on
  `._-` first: Postgres reads `budget.pdf` as one token, so without that a
  document was not reachable by either word in its own name.
- **Member addresses stay current.** A trigger on `auth.users` follows a
  confirmed email change into `user_emails`. It was written once at signup and
  never again, so the address an administrator would use to contact somebody
  went stale silently.
- **The platform has an imprint**, at `/legal/imprint`, in both locales, naming
  where a complaint about a *department* goes instead.
- **Truncated admin lists say so.** A list that came back exactly at its limit
  now carries a notice on its tab, and the audit log exports as CSV — every
  field quoted, formula-injection guarded, BOM for Excel. Until now the only
  way to keep any of the accountability record was a screenshot, and
  `purge_department()` empties the table.
- **Signup asks for the password twice** and offers to show it. The way back
  from one typed wrong is a reset mail to a department you have not joined
  yet — and for a founder, one that does not exist yet.

## Verification

`npm run typecheck`, `npm test` (118 Vitest, 249 SQL assertions across the two
schemas applied twice, two seed smoke runs) and `npm run build` all pass. The
new SQL assertions were checked red against the previous schema.

Not covered: nothing here was exercised against hosted Supabase Auth or Storage.
The Content-Type finding is reasoned from the upload path and Storage's
documented behaviour rather than reproduced against a live bucket, and the
concurrent mutual-ban race the settings lock now orders cannot be reproduced in
PGlite, which is single-backend.
